# Research references: actual methods and appropriate claims

1. **Ron Martin (2012), Regional economic resilience, hysteresis and recessionary shocks.** Journal of Economic Geography 12(1), 1–32. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/jeg/lbr019 . Framework: regional resistance, recovery, path dependence / hysteresis. Our adaptation is across industries and countries, measured chiefly in *industrial-production YoY growth* rather than regional employment. DO NOT claim a literal replication or output-level recovery if India only has YoY data.

2. **Martin, Sunley, Gardiner & Tyler (2016), How Regions React to Recessions: Resilience and the Role of Economic Structure.** Regional Studies 50(4), 561–585. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/00343404.2015.1136410 . Their research explicitly analyzes resistance and recoverability across recessions. Our flagship shock-profile is conceptually adapted, with a different unit of analysis and no assumed identical formula.

3. **Òscar Jordà (2005), Estimation and Inference of Impulse Responses by Local Projections.** American Economic Review 95(1), 161–182. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828053828518 . Our second flagship uses horizon-specific OLS as an **exploratory dynamic response association**, not a structurally identified shock or causal impulse response. Only publish coefficients and intervals if data are sufficient and specification is documented.

4. **Lutz Kilian (2009), Not All Oil Price Shocks Are Alike: Disentangling Demand and Supply Shocks in the Crude Oil Market.** American Economic Review 99(3), 1053–1069. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.99.3.1053 . Important conceptual warning: falling/rising Brent prices mix demand and supply origins. Our models cannot identify their origin from price alone.

5. **Acemoglu, Carvalho, Ozdaglar & Tahbaz-Salehi (2012), The Network Origins of Aggregate Fluctuations.** Econometrica 80(5), 1977–2016. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA9623 . Input-output inter-industry propagation. Cite as rationale for possible future OECD ICIO exposure, NOT claim implemented unless computed real IO matrix and linkages.

6. **OECD (2025), Supply Chain Resilience Review: Navigating Risks.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1787/94e3a8ea-en . Research context showing why simplistic isolation/relocalization does not necessarily improve resilience. Not a direct source of our sector-level shock coefficients.

7. **World Bank (2022), Commodity Markets Outlook, April 2022.** Overview: https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2022/04/26/food-and-energy-price-shocks-from-ukraine-war . Empirical context for 2022 energy and fertilizer price surge; not proof that all observed industry changes were caused by the invasion.

8. **Harvard Growth Lab, Atlas of Economic Complexity — Trade Data Methodology.** https://atlas.hks.harvard.edu/trade-data-methodology/ . Lessons: reconcile conflicting source reports, harmonize classification changes, record source reliability. **Harvard did not publish the historical industrial-resilience formula in this project**; credit Harvard for data-harmonization precedent, not claimed shock-model replication.

9. **UNIDO Statistics Portal — INDSTAT Revision 4.** https://stat.unido.org/data/database-descriptions . ISIC-based international industrial benchmarking for output/employment/VA. Not the flagship algorithm's original author.

## Citation rule
Every front-end paper reference needs the precise title, author, year, URL and *specific contribution*. Separate: `implemented method`, `inspired methodology`, `contextual reference`, and `optional data source`.
